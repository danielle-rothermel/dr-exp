### Deviations from the assignment, and why

1. **"Re-run the V2 sweep" is run as re-submitting V2's three arms.** V2 did not
   use a sweep: `canary_sweep.yaml` fixes `shrink: 0.4, perturb: 0.1` and sweeps
   `seed ∈ {0,1,2}`, so it is three copies of arm C, and V2's record documents
   running the canary as three `submit` calls instead. The V5 dedupe assertion —
   "zero new work items/attempts" — is about `work_key` identity, so it is
   evaluated against exactly the work V2 created. Running `canary_sweep.yaml`
   here would instead have created three *new* work items (its seeds 1 and 2 were
   never submitted to `val-v2`, and even seed 0 differs from every canary arm),
   which tests nothing about dedupe and would have burned three GPU jobs.
2. **Two dedupe probes, not one.** The same-run-key replay and a fresh-run-key
   submission exercise two different mechanisms and only the second is real
   `work_key` dedupe. Both are asserted; see assertions 1–8 and "Bearing on D-1".
3. **Four jobs, not three.** The assignment says to submit the fourth config
   "while a bounded worker runs two others", which needs two occupiers plus the
   priority job. A **fourth**, priority-100 job was added as the control: without
   a competing READY item, "admitted before any READY priority-100 item" is
   vacuously true. The waiter is submitted *before* the priority job so that
   FIFO, submission order, and `stage_execution_id` order all predict the
   opposite outcome from priority — assertion 13.
4. **New seeds for the four load jobs.** Re-using the canary configs would have
   collided with V1–V4 work keys and their shared workspaces (the cross-campaign
   workspace sharing V2/V3/V4 all recorded), forcing another archive-and-clear
   incident and making "no resume happened" ambiguous. Seeds 11/12/13/7 give
   four fresh `work_key`s with empty workspaces. The YAMLs are generated from
   dr-vision's committed configs by a single `sed` on the `seed:` line and are
   archived with the run; dr-vision was not modified.
5. **`--max-jobs 4` rather than an operator SIGTERM.** The worker reaches a
   terminal state on its own once all four items finish. Nothing in V5 depends
   on shutdown behaviour (that is V4).

## Bearing on D-1 (recorded in V2)

V2 filed **D-1**: a second `submit` under an existing run key is silently
discarded while reporting success, because `SubmissionResult.work_keys` is built
from the locally constructed `members` list
(`src/dr_exp/platform/submission.py:84-87`) while the printed counts come from
the receipt (`src/dr_exp/cli/main.py:289-297`).

V5's assertion 1 hit the same path from the benign direction and confirms the
mechanism precisely. Re-submitting `two_phase_random.yaml` under run key
`canary` printed `1 member(s), 1 new, 0 reused` — the counts of the **original**
registration, replayed — while writing nothing. Here that misreport is harmless
(the work genuinely exists and the message happens to describe reality), but it
is the identical code path that is dangerous when the config differs.

This **narrows D-1 usefully**: the bug is not that dr-exp fails to deduplicate.
Deduplication works correctly and is reported correctly whenever the submission
actually reaches dr-platform's dedupe (assertion 4: `0 new, 1 reused`). The
defect is confined to the *closed-run replay* path, where dr-exp echoes a stale
receipt instead of the current submission's fate. A fix that reports the receipt
faithfully — and reports the locally computed `work_keys` only when they are the
ones the receipt describes — resolves both the silent-discard case V2 found and
the misleading-but-harmless case V5 found.

D-2 (worker `--campaign` does not scope admission, V2) is not exercised here:
V5 deliberately ran with no other campaign's work non-terminal. D-2 and D-3 from
V3 are untouched.

## Defects

**None new.** Every V5 assertion passed. Dedupe, priority admission ordering, and
both `boost` paths behave exactly as `docs/plans/validate-with-dr-vision.md`
describes.

Two behaviours are worth naming as *not* defects:

- **A reused work item keeps its original `origin_run_key`.** Item 12 stays
  `canary` after joining `v5-dedupe-probe`. `platform_run_memberships` records
  the full set of runs a work item belongs to, so no information is lost;
  `origin_run_key` is the run that created it, which is the only answer that can
  be stable under dedupe.
- **`boost` on a READY item reports `1 stage(s) updated` with no workflow
  touched.** Correct: no attempt workflow exists until admission, and the new
  priority is picked up by the next admission pass from
  `platform_stage_executions.priority` (`dr_platform/admission/runner.py:683-687`
  orders by `(priority, rank, stage_execution_id)`).

- **A third 2-wide measurement, and it lands between V2 and V4.** Round 1 at
  33 333 samples: medians 8.20, 8.09, 7.04, 7.33 s/epoch against the solo
  baseline of 6.11 s (V1) / 6.10 s (V2 arm A) — **1.15x to 1.34x**, i.e. a
  15–34% per-epoch penalty. V2 measured 20–23% and V4 measured 42% on the same
  profile and subset. V4's record asked for a third data point before `mini`'s
  `worker_concurrency` is fixed from these numbers; V5 supplies one, and it
  argues V4's 42% is the outlier rather than the new normal. V5 still was not
  *designed* as a throughput experiment (jobs start and finish at staggered
  times, so pairings change), so the open item V4 raised — one deliberate A/B —
  stands. Even at the worst observed penalty, 2-wide remains a makespan win.
- **The two jobs sharing a slot track each other closely.** Occupiers A and B
  ran paired for their whole round 1: medians 8.20 and 8.09 s, means 8.09 and
  8.03 s. The waiter and the priority job were likewise paired for most of
  theirs: medians 7.04 and 7.33 s. The same even GPU sharing V2 and V4 observed.
- **Makespan.** Four jobs, 224 epochs, 1 353 s of summed epoch time, completed
  in 698 s wall clock (`15:29:44` first admission → `15:41:22` last terminal).
- **Round 0 is cheaper than V4's.** Medians 3.45–4.35 s at 16 666 samples
  against V4's 3.96/4.31 s, consistent with the same machine under the same
  concurrency.
- **Priority costs nothing at runtime.** The priority-10 job's per-epoch times
  (median 7.33 s round 1) are indistinguishable from its slot-mate's (7.04 s).
  Priority orders admission only; it does not change scheduling once running,
  which is the intended behaviour and worth knowing before anyone reaches for
  `boost` to speed a job up.
